# Content guide

How to write for this site. Short sentences. Real construction words. Nothing inflated.

Say what was done and where. Skip anything that sounds like an advertisement.

## Voice

Grounded, confident, practical, specific.

Write: `Exterior masonry repair and reconstruction.`
Not: `We elevate the built environment through visionary craftsmanship.`

Write: `Completed in South Chicago.`
Not: `Proudly transforming communities one dream at a time.`

Avoid these words: revolutionary, cutting edge, seamless, unlock, elevate, empower, next generation, best in class, world class, transforming spaces, bringing visions to life, building dreams.

Never state a license, certification, years in business, crew size, number of completed projects, award, client name, review score, union status or guarantee unless it is true and you can show it. The default copy states none of these.

## What the company says it does

The site describes the work as interior work, exterior work, renovation and improvements, project coordination and subcontractor management. Work done by subcontractors (roofing, plumbing, electrical, HVAC and so on) is described as coordinated or managed, not as a Jacinto Construction specialty. Keep project descriptions consistent with that: `roofing by a subcontractor on the same schedule`, not `we roofed it`.

## Project titles

What it was, in plain words. Four to six words.

- `Two-Flat Masonry Restoration`
- `Bungalow Kitchen and Rear Addition`
- `Corner Storefront Build-Out`
- `Detached Garage and Concrete Flatwork`

Not: `Beautiful Dream Kitchen Transformation`.

## Year

The year the work was finished. That is the only date the site uses; projects are browsed by year.

## Location

One line, the area and the state, not the address. `South Chicago, IL`, `Chicago, IL`, `Evergreen Park, IL`. Never a street address or a client name.

## Project value

Optional. The contract value in whole dollars, digits only: `185000`. The site shows `$185,000`. Leave it empty when the number is private or unknown; nothing is shown in that case. It stays in US dollars on the Spanish site.

## Description

One field. Two to four short paragraphs. Leave an empty line between them. A good order:

1. What the building was and what the client needed.
2. What was done, in the order it happened, and which parts were subcontracted.
3. Anything worth knowing: matched mortar, met an opening date, kept the family in the house during the work.

The first sentences of the first paragraph become the short preview on the home page and in the lists, automatically. So lead with the work, not with the weather: `Tuckpointing, lintel replacement and a rebuilt parapet on a brick two-flat.` is a good first sentence.

Numbers are welcome when they are real: `six windows`, `12 by 16 foot addition`, `seven weeks`.

## Scope of work (optional)

Under Optional details. One item per line, three to eight lines. Nouns first.

```
Grind and repoint, front and side elevations
Six steel lintels replaced
Rear parapet rebuilt with salvaged brick
New limestone caps
```

## Photo alt text (optional)

Every photo gets `{Project name}, project photo {N}` on its own. Replace it when you have a minute: one short sentence saying what is in the photo, for a person who cannot see it. It is also what search engines read.

- `Front elevation of a brick two-flat after repointing`
- `New steel column on a poured footing in the basement`
- `Kitchen with white cabinets looking into the rear addition`

Not: `IMG_4521`, `photo`, `nice work`.

## Captions (optional)

Most photos do not need one. Use a caption when it adds a fact the photo does not show.

- `Mortar matched to the original`
- `Framing before drywall`
- `Parapet taken down to sound brick`

## Before and after (optional)

Under Optional photo details, mark one photo as "Before photo" and one as "After photo", taken from the same spot if possible. They appear as a pair. Keep captions to `Before` and `After` or a few words more.

## Which photos to use

- Real jobs only. No stock photos.
- Mix wide shots and details. Include at least one photo of the finished work.
- Phone photos are fine, straight from the phone, HEIC included. Shoot in daylight, hold the phone level, do not use filters.
- Portrait and landscape both work; the layout adapts.
- The first photo you pick is the cover, so pick the best one first or set the cover afterwards.
- Four to fifteen photos per project is plenty.

## Home page

- Headline: one or two short sentences. `Built with care. Made to last.` Each sentence gets its own line automatically.
- Intro: one paragraph, what the company does and where.

## About page

- Opening line: one or two sentences about the company, in the third person. Do not add a biography, training or history that has not been confirmed.
- Text: three short paragraphs. Who, how the work runs, what a client can expect.
- What we do: one service per line. Only services actually offered. The defaults are interior work, exterior work, renovation and improvements, project coordination, subcontractor management.
- Where we work: the service area setting, for example `The Chicago area`. Do not name territories that have not been confirmed.

## Contact text

One or two sentences. Tell people the fastest way to reach the company.

## Search description

One or two sentences, under 160 characters if possible, naming the company, the city and the main services. This is what shows under the site name in Google.

## Spanish

Every text field has an optional Spanish twin, in the Spanish translation section of the project and next to each field in Settings. Write it as a Spanish speaker would say it, not word for word. If a Spanish field is empty, Spanish readers see the English text, so partial translation is fine and nothing has to be translated before publishing. Keep names of neighbourhoods and building types as locals say them.
